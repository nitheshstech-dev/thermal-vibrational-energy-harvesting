# Project Explanation

## What problem did we solve?

Industrial machines and vehicles generate mechanical vibration and heat that are normally lost. Our project explored recovering part of this waste energy and converting it into useful electrical energy.

## What did we build?

We built a physical proof-of-concept that combines two harvesting methods:

- Piezoelectric sensors for vibration energy
- Thermoelectric modules for thermal energy

The harvested energy is conditioned, stored in a 12 V battery and monitored using Arduino-based electronics.

## Why is the system hybrid?

Vibration and heat do not remain constant all the time. Using two sources improves the opportunity for energy recovery because one source may still contribute when the other is weak.

## Why is this useful for EVs?

The future concept is to mount suitable harvesting elements at locations where EVs already experience useful vibration or temperature gradients. The recovered energy would be supplementary power, reducing some load on the primary electrical system rather than replacing the traction battery.

## Why is this useful for industry?

Motors, pumps, conveyors and other machinery continuously create vibration and heat. A scaled system could use that energy to support monitoring electronics and low-power industrial IoT nodes.

## Future vision

The project can evolve from a college prototype into a smarter energy-recovery platform by adding:

1. Higher-efficiency harvesting materials.
2. Better thermal management.
3. Smarter power conditioning and energy management.
4. ESP8266/ESP32-based remote monitoring.
5. AI-based vibration/temperature analytics for predictive maintenance.
6. Vehicle-level EV integration studies.
7. Wireless energy-transfer research for future charging applications.

## Interview answer
